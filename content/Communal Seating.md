At our standard dinners, guests will be prompted to sit down as we did at [[the Mountain School]]. 

> At meal times, students and faculty trickle into the dining room. Starting at the table closest to the kitchen, and moving counterclockwise, people sit down at the tables which are yet unfilled. If a table is filled, they move to the next table. You don’t choose which table to sit at, or the people you wish to sit with – you come into the room and take a seat at the table which is filling up.**

We come in, and without attempt form diverse and mostly spurious judgements.

Within a week or two, each student has sat with each other. You've sat with the student you thought was "too cool for you" and the student you thought was "not cool enough". You've sat with the head of school, farmers, maintenance staff, teachers, and faculty children. This does not beget one unified social graph, but it extends a layer of shared concern, interest, and trust amongst the people of a place.  

More, each person at the table will inevitably do something for another at the table. For example, someone may grab the water pitcher and another may pour it. It is not only the eating with people, but extending small acts of service to one another. One, it suggests that each person is willing to act in service of the whole. Two, it creates a pattern of gift and reception, which generates trust and respect. Each of these small actions press up against our judgements, and replace (==complicate?==) them with feelings of social trust.
