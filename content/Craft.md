#### Craft and Belonging

This is a restaurant that asks: how do we design for belonging?

Belonging is not only about human relations, but relations broadly. Going back home, walking in familiar territory, etc...

To build relationships of belonging


Craft is where making and knowing come together (Richard Sennett). When we grind, brew, and serve coffee, experience condenses into an act of care.

The receiver, in attending to the act, relates taste to craft. They experience that form of care in its totality: taste, warmth, the materiality of the cup, and more.

Our epistemic frame joins experience with craft, embedding beauty and care into perception.

Craft, then, can make the relations we draw between things meaningful, as these are relations of care, and thus bring us into togetherness/oneness, which is the basis of belonging.

#### The Values of the Craftspeople

Looking at the note of the craftspeople themselves, we learn about what matters. For us as people, and for the restaurant. 

I've looked at the over 100 craftspeople I'm considering working with, and looked at their sites, writings, and interviews. Here is... ==stuff here==

This is, roughly, of how often they came up

==how does the bear on the restaurants and design values?==

###### Made by Hands

Most talk about a "need" to make things with their hands. This is embedded in language, with words like hand-made and hand-craft. This also includes using "hand tools". The person is involved in their work, the processes are directed by the body, rather than the outcome of a machine. 
###### Beautiful and Functional

Nearly all had some language that held up both the beautiful and the functional. Attractive and useful. Technical and aesthetic. It varied 

> "My goal is to craft objects of significance" [Sean Fitz Forge](https://www.seanfitzforge.com/)

Significance, generally and to the user. Generally, in faithful and respectful interpretation of the material . For the user, something the use and love. 

###### Material

> "When I begin a project, I have a vision of what I want to create and a rough idea of the steps involved. But standing before the anvil and fire, it is the metal that guides the blacksmith as much as the blacksmith who guides the metal.  This is what makes each day at the anvil a real joy." [Knox Forge](http://www.knoxtrailforge.com/)

The material guides the maker. The item is the collaborative process of the maker and material (one may also add the tradition or the hand tools to this squad). 

The great craftsperson listens to their material and knows it well. A great craft is of the material and the maker. 
###### Tradition

Care about the traditions of their craft, making their craft traditionally, or using traditional techniques. 

Several said a variant of "the same items that are prized today, that's how I make mine"  or "I care about making things that matched the quality of what I see". 

Several more spoke of just loving "old stuff". 

###### Longevity

Care about making an item that lasts. 

Several more used the language of "durability". To be well made is to last a while, as long as the material may allow for. 

They speak of an item which can be used and passed on. Items that last hold the patina and patterned use of the people who used them. It should not only wear the mark of the maker, but more importantly the owner. It should reflect love and repeated use. 

These connect us with the maker, across time, and others, often family members or friends, who have since passed. 

Several more use the related word of "timeless", speaking not only of the material piece holding up to aging, but of its general qualities lasting.


Care not only about the longevity of the item, but I'd harken to say the longevity of themselves.

> "I believe expressing our energy to shape matter imparts it with the indelible mark of our spirits." Sean Fitz Forge

###### In-house

Making everything in house. They touch each part of the process, so that every part is made for the purpose of the thing. 

More, for some crafts, the tools are made of the same material, and thus the person make his or her own tools (i.e. blacksmith)
