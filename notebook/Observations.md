**April 11th**

There are several places to sit outside the restaurant. There are the stacks of wood which contain the trees. The bench outside, on the right bay window (looking out), provides an opposing seat to one of them. A man sits on the hardly-a-step-step, which leads into the restaurant, so he is at a more appropriate height for their daughter. The wife sits on the wood, across him. The daughter is sandwiched in between. 
- I think we should put a set of chairs, or a bench, under the other bay window. Though, with the signs, maybe it's not a good idea. 

People stop to look at the signs. Two QR codes, for email and instagram. A handwritten note, introducing myself. A weekly update. Passerbys stop and read, heads tilted in. You can tell when someone reads the handwritten note, versus the printed text, because my handwriting is poor enough to make eyes squint and brows burrow. 

Some, though not most, then come to the door and push their head up against the glass. A few are startled by my being in here. Most smile and wave. I do the same. If someone lingers, I open the door and say hello. I sit by the window and work at my laptop. I observe too.

[[The shopkeeper is the most intelligent person on the block]]. I grew up in this neighborhood, a few blocks from the restaurant. Yet, I think I've learned more in the last 10 days, and even in the first few, than I have in my time living here. The intelligence is a kind of situated spatial knowledge. Most of it is about the building and the block. 

I've met many upstairs neighbors. Liv and Oliver were introduced to me by Willa. They in turn introduced me to Max and Maia. A dozen others have said hello. There is a teacher, with a young daughter. Amanda, in her 70s, was born in this building and still lives in it. She's a pianist, wear's heart shaped pink glasses, and walks here low-to-the-ground wirey dog by the restaurant most days. She tells me her boyfriend is a BCC reporter, in London. She wants to play piano in here and suggests that we have a garnet-bedazzled piano. Anywhere in the restaurant is fine with her. A pudgy middle aged man smokes across the street, several times a day. An older couple, too pudgy, walks their dog and takes out the trash. They live in the complex across the street, with the arched green door. 

This morning, JR, the owner of Laundry and Latte, offers me a coffee as I pas by at 7:45 to the restaurant. 

We receive a package from Empire Syrups, with Sorghum syrup, from a soon-to-retire Cornell professor, and the only remaining farmer/producer who makes this wildly tasty syrup.
